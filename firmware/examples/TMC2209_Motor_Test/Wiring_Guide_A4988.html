A4988 Motor Test: Wiring Guide

ESP32-S3 dev kit, A4988 stepper driver module, 12 V supply, one stepper motor. Uses the same sketch (TMC2209_Motor_Test.ino) and the same test page as the TMC2209 guide, with no code changes.

Before you wire anything
  • Wire with everything switched off: USB unplugged and the 12 V supply unplugged.
  • Never plug in or pull out the motor while 12 V is on. That can destroy the driver instantly.
  • Fit the 100 µF capacitor across VMOT and GND. On the A4988 this is not optional: without it, voltage spikes from the supply wires can kill the chip.
  • Measure the supply with a multimeter before connecting it. The A4988 needs 8 V to 35 V on VMOT; use 12 V.
  • 12 V goes only to VMOT. Never let 12 V touch the ESP32 or the driver's VDD pin.
  • Set the current limit (section 7) before running the motor for long. The A4988 gets hot.
  • Keep the motor detached from the valve for this test.

1. Wiring diagram

The rounded labels GND and 3V3 mean "connect this point to ground" and "connect this point to 3.3 V". All GND points are one net. The driver's two GND pins are joined on its board, which is what links the ESP32 ground to the 12 V negative. Both must be connected.

Leave empty: MS3.

Go by the names printed on the driver, not by position. On the usual module, looking at the top (the side with the small adjusting screw), one row is ENABLE, MS1, MS2, MS3, RESET, SLEEP, STEP, DIR and the other is VMOT, GND, 2B, 2A, 1A, 1B, VDD, GND. Many boards have the names printed only on the underside, so check before plugging in. A driver plugged in the wrong way round puts 12 V on the logic pins and is usually destroyed.

On the ESP32-S3 dev kit use the pins labelled 13, 14, 15, 3V3 and GND. These are GPIO numbers, not positions counted along the header.

3. Switching on, in order

  1. Check every connection against the list with the power off.
  2. Plug the ESP32 into the computer by USB.
  3. Then switch on the 12 V supply.
  4. To switch off: 12 V first, USB last. Only touch the motor wires after 12 V is off.

If the 12 V comes from a USB-C PD trigger board: its switches must be set for 12 V, and the charger must actually offer 12 V. If the board falls back to 5 V the A4988 will not work at all, because it needs at least 8 V.

4. Running the test

The sketch already on the board works for the A4988 as it is. Nothing needs to be uploaded again.

Test page: double-click Start_Motor_Tester.command in this folder. It opens http://localhost:8765, where you can set the speed, ramp and angle, turn continuously and run a back-and-forth test, with a STOP button. Leave "Microstepping" on 1/8 if you wired MS1 and MS2 to 3V3.

Or by keyboard, in a serial monitor at 115200 baud:

cd ~/Desktop/TMC2209_Motor_Test

Only one program can use the USB port, so close the serial monitor before starting the test page, and the other way round.

5. Fault finding

Use a multimeter on DC volts, black probe on a driver GND pin, and measure on the driver's own pins, not on the wire ends.

#MeasureShould readIf not
1VMOT to GNDabout 12 V (at least 8 V)No motor power. Check the supply, its polarity and the two power wires.
2VDD to GND3.3 VThe 3V3 wire or the ESP32 GND wire is missing or loose.
3SLEEP and RESET to GNDboth about 3.3 VThe jumper between them is missing. This is the most common reason an A4988 does nothing.
4ENABLE to GND, nothing runningabout 3 VThe pull-up resistor or the GPIO15 wire is misplaced.
5ENABLE to GND, after typing h0 V for 20 secondsGPIO15 is not reaching ENABLE.
6DIR to GND, after typing r, then after f3.3 V after r, 0 V after fGPIO14 is not reaching DIR. The same kind of fault is then likely on STEP.
7Turn the shaft by hand during hlocked firmlyIf 1 to 5 are right and it is still loose: motor wires (section 6), current set to almost zero (section 7), or the driver is damaged.
  • Locked during h but no turning on f: power and motor are fine. The STEP wire (GPIO13 to STEP) is the problem.
  • Buzzes or twitches but does not turn: the coil pairs are mixed up, or the current is set too low.
  • Turns, then stops and starts again by itself: the driver is overheating and protecting itself. Lower the current or add the heat sink.
  • Loose pins: the driver's header pins must be soldered.

The A4988 is much louder than the Silent2209: a whine or hum while the motor turns or holds is normal.

6. Finding the motor's coil pairs

  1. Disconnect the motor from the driver (12 V off first).
  2. Set the multimeter to resistance. Two wires that show a few ohms between them are one coil. Wires from different coils show no connection.
  3. No meter: touch two motor wires together and turn the shaft by hand. If it is noticeably harder to turn, those two are a pair.
  4. Put one pair on 1A and 1B and the other pair on 2A and 2B. Swapping the two wires of one pair only reverses the direction.

7. Setting the current limit

The small screw on the driver sets the motor current. With 12 V and 3.3 V on, measure from the metal top of the screw to GND. That voltage is VREF. The current depends on the two small current-sense resistors on the board, so read the marking printed on them:

Marking on the resistorsMotor currentVREF for 0.8 A
R100 (0.1 Ω, most low-cost modules)VREF × 1.250.64 V
R068 (0.068 Ω)VREF × 1.840.44 V
R050 (0.05 Ω)VREF × 2.50.32 V

Stay at or below the current printed on the motor or in its data sheet. Without a heat sink keep it to about 1 A; the chip's absolute limit is 2 A with good cooling. Turn the screw in small steps with a plastic or insulated screwdriver. Unlike the Silent2209, the A4988 keeps full current flowing while the motor stands still, so the driver and motor warm up during a hold.
