TMC2209 Motor Test: Wiring Guide

ESP32-S3 dev kit, FYSETC Silent2209 V3.0 stepper driver, 12 V supply, one stepper motor. For the sketch TMC2209_Motor_Test.ino in this folder.

Before you wire anything
  • Wire with everything switched off: USB unplugged and the 12 V supply unplugged.
  • Never plug in or pull out the motor while 12 V is on. That can destroy the driver instantly.
  • Measure the supply with a multimeter before connecting it to the driver. It must read about 12 V, with the polarity you expect.
  • 12 V goes only to the driver's VM pin. Never let 12 V touch the ESP32 or the driver's VIO pin.
  • Keep the motor detached from the valve for this test.

1. Wiring diagram

The rounded labels GND and 3V3 mean "connect this point to ground" and "connect this point to 3.3 V". All GND points are one net. The driver's two GND pins are joined on its board, which is what links the ESP32 ground to the 12 V negative. Both must be connected.

Go by the names printed on the driver, not by position. Looking at the printed side with EN at the top left, the left row is EN, MS1, MS2, SP, RX, TX, STEP, DIR and the right row is VM, GND, A2, A1, B1, B2, VIO, GND. If the board sits in a breadboard with the printed side down, the rows are mirrored. A driver plugged in the wrong way round puts 12 V on the logic pins and is usually destroyed.

On the ESP32-S3 dev kit use the pins labelled 13, 14, 15, 3V3 and GND. These are GPIO numbers, not positions counted along the header.

3. Switching on, in order

  1. Check every connection against the list with the power off.
  2. Plug the ESP32 into the computer by USB.
  3. Then switch on the 12 V supply.
  4. To switch off: 12 V first, USB last. Only touch the motor wires after 12 V is off.

If the 12 V comes from a USB-C PD trigger board: its switches must be set for 12 V, and the charger must actually offer 12 V. Many USB-C chargers offer 5, 9, 15 and 20 V but skip 12 V, and the board then gives a different voltage. Measure the output terminals to be sure.

5. Fault finding: shaft turns freely and nothing happens

A shaft that stays loose means the driver is not powering the motor coils. The ESP32 and the code have been checked: the board answers every command with the right timing. Work down this list with a multimeter on DC volts, black probe on a driver GND pin, and measure on the driver's own pins, not on the wire ends.

#MeasureShould readIf not
1VM to GNDabout 12 VNo motor power. Check the supply, its polarity, the PD board setting and the two power wires.
2VIO to GND3.3 VThe 3V3 wire or the ESP32 GND wire is missing or loose.
3VREF hole to GND (12 V on)roughly 0.6 to 1.5 V0 V: VM is not reaching the chip, or the current screw is at minimum. Turn the screw a little and measure again.
4EN to GND, nothing running3.3 VEN wire or the pull-up resistor is misplaced.
5EN to GND, after typing h0 V for 20 secondsGPIO15 is not reaching EN, or EN is tied straight to 3V3 without the resistor.
6DIR to GND, after typing r, then after f3.3 V after r, 0 V after fGPIO14 is not reaching DIR. The same kind of fault is then likely on STEP.
7Turn the shaft by hand during hclearly stiffer than with the power offIf 1 to 5 are right and it is still loose: motor wires (section 6), or the driver is damaged.
  • Stiff during h but no turning on f: power and motor are fine. The STEP wire (GPIO13 to STEP) is the problem.
  • Buzzes or twitches but does not turn: the coil pairs are mixed up, or the current is set too low.
  • Loose pins: the driver's header pins must be soldered. Pins that are only pushed through the holes make no reliable contact.

6. Finding the motor's coil pairs

  1. Disconnect the motor from the driver (12 V off first).
  2. Set the multimeter to resistance. Two wires that show a few ohms between them are one coil. Wires from different coils show no connection.
  3. No meter: touch two motor wires together and turn the shaft by hand. If it is noticeably harder to turn, those two are a pair.
  4. Put one pair on A1 and A2 and the other pair on B1 and B2. Swapping the two wires of one pair only reverses the direction.

7. Setting the current limit

The small screw on the driver sets the motor current. With 12 V on and the motor not being plugged or unplugged, measure VREF to GND. Motor current is roughly VREF × 0.7 amps (for example 1.0 V gives about 0.7 A). Stay at or below the current printed on the motor or in its data sheet. Turn the screw in small steps with a plastic or insulated screwdriver. A driver that gets too hot to touch needs less current or a heat sink.
